# Temporal contract skill: ownership, completion, and effects

Purpose: complete the original maintenance task while checking timing and ownership where they affect its behavior. The task and observed project evidence define the contract.

S1 Applicability. Check whether work may finish after its initiating access or consumer changes, callbacks may run during setup or cleanup, or one result feeds effects with different validity rules. If none applies, briefly record why and continue the maintenance task.

S2 Establish evidence. Read the entry point, producer or decoder, completion path, ownership transitions, cancellation and scheduling APIs, and affected consumers. Inspect adjacent implementations for supported patterns, not automatic specifications. Record source locations for consequential facts. Separate task requirements, observed behavior, hypotheses, and unavailable evidence. Do not infer a contract from names, familiar framework patterns, or a passing self-written check.

S3 Define identities and effects. Where the source distinguishes them, track the resource, access, operation or attempt, and accepted result. For each effect, identify its owner and the condition for applying it at completion: stored records, current display, delivery, notification, loading state, scheduled work, and cleanup may differ. Determine whether cancellation blocks future input, cancels callbacks, aborts running work, or only releases a consumer. Do not make one validity guard govern every effect without evidence.

S4 State the intermediate-state contract. Before changing an asynchronous flow, specify what users and consumers should observe while work is pending, while a retry is scheduled, during a retry, after readiness, and after close or disposal. Check that progress flags and visible output agree with the actual phase. A pending response must not be treated as final completion; scheduling more work must not by itself imply that the overall operation has stopped. Resolve the intended state from the task and source; mark unsupported details unknown.

S5 Trace each handoff. Follow the order of listener attachment, work start, handle creation, ownership publication, callback execution, and cleanup. At calls into external or user code, consider synchronous reentry. Recheck ownership after such calls before publishing handles or effects. Account for an immediate result and a callback that runs before its scheduling call returns. Ensure an old operation cannot overwrite ownership created by a reentrant access.

S6 Compare concrete event orders. Using project APIs, exercise ordinary pending-to-ready progress; completion after close, switch, or disposal; reuse of a resource under a new access; replacement or retry within an access; and synchronous reentry where supported. Compare normal and reversed completion order when results may settle out of order. Derive ordering from supported identities or arrival sequence, without inventing a payload schema. Observe each relevant effect separately, including intermediate progress state and any retained effect of superseded work. For scheduled work, cover cancellation and reopening while a callback is pending or running.

S7 Preserve established behavior. Keep public interfaces, unrelated consumers, ordinary streaming, records, diagnostics, cleanup, and adjacent behavior consistent with the task. Distinguish access end from attempt end: reuse and retry may have different ownership rules. Do not assume that closing a view cancels background work or removes stored effects. Use evidence to decide; otherwise report the uncertainty.

S8 Repair and verify. Implement the smallest supported change that completes the maintenance task. Run available relevant checks and inspect their actual output. Add focused checks for consequential untested orders and, when applicable, at least one boundary or reentry path. Assert observable intermediate and final states, separate effects, and ownership; a check that only reaches code is insufficient. A local harness proves only the paths it drives. Syntax checks prove syntax, and unavailable dependencies leave behavior unverified. If a check fails, revise the repair and rerun the relevant check. Finish with the maintenance result, verification performed, and explicit remaining uncertainty; contract analysis alone is not completion.
