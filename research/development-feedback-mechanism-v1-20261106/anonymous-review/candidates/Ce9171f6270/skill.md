# Temporal contract skill: ownership, completion, and effects

Purpose: complete the original maintenance task. Apply this skill only where the task and visible project evidence support it. It does not add product requirements or replace implementation with a contract discussion.

S1 Applicability. Look for work that may complete after its initiating access, operation, or consumer changes. Also look for synchronous calls or callbacks that can change ownership before the initiating call returns, and for distinct effects such as durable history and a current view. If none apply, note the evidence and continue the maintenance task.

S2 Establish evidence. Inspect the task entry point; producer, decoder, or dependency; registration and startup order; completion callback; cancellation and ownership transitions; and every affected effect consumer. Inspect adjacent consumers of shared APIs and public interfaces. Cite source locations. Separate explicit task requirements, source facts, hypotheses, and unavailable evidence. Do not infer guarantees from names, comments alone, or plausible input formats.

S3 Trace identities and effects. Identify the resource, access or visit, physical attempt, and accepted value or work where the source distinguishes them. Trace when each identity becomes visible, is replaced or retired, and is checked. Determine whether cancellation stops new input, cancels work already started, removes a listener, or only suppresses one effect. For each effect independently, state its required ownership and ordering conditions from the task and source. A current view may have a different validity rule from retained history; establish that distinction rather than applying one guard to all effects.

S4 Build event orders. Compare ordinary startup and completion; completion after close or switch; same-resource reuse; replacement or retry when present; and normal versus reversed completion of ordered results. Check synchronous emission during startup and synchronous reentry from externally supplied callbacks, including callbacks triggered by cleanup. At each boundary, ask whether an old call can resume and install, overwrite, publish, remove, or cancel work belonging to a newer owner. Record expected effects separately for each order and each consumer. Use only supported APIs and source-backed result shapes.

S5 Preserve behavior. Keep existing public interfaces, unrelated consumers of shared helpers, and task-specified lifecycle, diagnostic, record, and cleanup behavior. Treat background obligations, rollback, permission withdrawal, and dependencies that suppress completion as source-dependent exceptions. If evidence is absent, leave the point unresolved instead of inventing a universal persistence or cancellation rule.

S6 Repair and verify. Make the smallest source-supported maintenance change, or explain with evidence why no change is needed or why work remains incomplete. Run relevant available checks and inspect their actual output. Add targeted checks only for a concrete untested risk. Drive the important event orders through the real entry point and assert each affected effect independently, including an ownership change during startup or cleanup when supported. Include both ordinary use and an adversarial order; verify subsequent use and adjacent behavior where relevant. Revise when a check fails. Report failed or unrun checks. A passing local harness proves only the orders it drove, and a contract explanation alone does not complete the repair.
